Round 1 format

Lean canvas

Problem top 3

  • Learning doesn't lead to earning. Only 5.6% of rural youth aged 14–18 take any vocational course, and youth unemployment is 10.2%. Students don't know what they're good at or which path fits them.
  • Opportunities are scattered. Scholarships, schemes, apprenticeships, mentors and jobs sit on separate English-first portals, so eligible students never apply.
  • Existing tools don't fit village reality. 25% can't read a Class 2 text fluently, only 19.8% of girls own a smartphone, there are 48 rural internet subscriptions per 100 people, and 1,04,125 schools have a single teacher.

Existing alternatives

  • DIKSHA, SWAYAM, PM eVidya, YouTube for learning
  • NSP, myScheme, NAPS, NCS portals for money and jobs
  • Paid coaching in the nearest town
  • Cyber-café agents who charge to fill forms
  • Advice from relatives and seniors

Gap: none of these connect discover, learn and earn, and none work by voice offline.

Solution

  • Didi, a helper in the phone: a friendly AI character who talks, listens and does the paperwork alongside the student.
  • Discover: voice or text chat with Didi plus picture games → profile of interests, skills and goals → career options across STEM, vocational and non-STEM, drawn from the full NCO-2015 occupation list, not a few popular careers.
  • Learn: personal pathway of what to learn next, offline micro-lessons, relevant competitive exams, AI doubt helper.
  • Opportunity: grounded matching to scholarships, schemes, apprenticeships, mentors and employers, and Didi helps fill the application.
  • Facilitator mode: one teacher runs a whole class, spots talent, recommends students for opportunities, and builds modules offered free, per module or by subscription.

Key metrics

  • Weekly active learners: target 60% of onboarded students in the pilot
  • 30-day retention: target 40%
  • Share of sessions done offline: target 50%+
  • Lessons completed: target 3+ per learner per week
  • Applications: target 30% of eligible students start one, 60% of those submit
  • Apprenticeships, placements and mentor matches secured
  • Active facilitators and their module earnings
  • Cost per learner per year

Targets are our pilot goals, to be measured, not results.

Unique value proposition

“Speak in your own language, learn even on 2G or no signal, and find the money and jobs you qualify for. One app, one mic button.”

High-level concept

Duolingo + myScheme + Naukri, voice-first, for a village student on a ₹6,000 phone.

Unfair advantage

  • Village mesh sharing: one phone downloads a pack, the whole class gets it over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct at zero data cost.
  • Teacher-made dialect content that grows with every facilitator.
  • Verified scheme knowledge graph with eligibility rules and sources.
  • Outcome data from learning to placement that governments and CSR funders need.

Channels

  • Outbound: govt schools and ITIs through teachers, state education and skill departments, NGO and CSR partners, Common Service Centres, Panchayats
  • Inbound: WhatsApp referral links, mesh sharing between students, Play Store, preload deals with budget phone brands

Innovation and originality · 25%

What's new in Raahi

The brief says education, skills, mentorship and work "exist as disconnected ecosystems". Raahi's core idea is to connect them for one student, by voice, on a cheap phone and a slow network.

Connects the ecosystems

Pathway Graph

One graph built from public data links each career to skills, lessons, SWAYAM and Skill India courses, exams, scholarships, apprenticeships, employers and mentors. Didi walks the student along it one step at a time.

Works on 2G

Text-only voice AI

Speech is understood on the phone and only text goes to the cloud AI, about 2 KB per question. Full AI help works where voice bots that upload audio fail.

A second person in the phone

Didi

One friendly character runs onboarding, lessons, answers and forms, so a first-time user always knows who to talk to. She is also the accessibility layer: everything she shows, she says.

Zero data cost

Village mesh

One phone downloads a signed pack and shares it with the class over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct. Content spreads without anyone paying for data.

Teachers earn

Teacher as entrepreneur

Facilitators build modules in their own dialect and choose free, per-module or subscription pricing. Didi flags hidden talent from quiz patterns so teachers can recommend students.

Proof employers trust

Verifiable Skill Passport

Skills earned become digitally signed credentials (W3C Verifiable Credentials format). An employer scans a QR code and checks it, even without internet.

Build order

  1. Knowledge base. Collect schemes, scholarships, apprenticeships, courses and careers from the public datasets listed below into structured tables with eligibility rules and source links. No synthetic records.
  2. App shell and voice. Kotlin app with the three-tab layout, language picker, offline speech-to-text and text-to-speech, Didi onboarding.
  3. Learn offline. Lesson and quiz player, content packs in the local database, progress tracking and streaks.
  4. Earn matcher and AI. On-device eligibility engine; cloud AI service with retrieval over the knowledge base and cited answers.
  5. Sync and facilitator mode. Outbox sync, shared-phone profiles, teacher view, class pack sharing.
  6. Dashboard and demo. Next.js org dashboard and a scripted demo: onboard, learn offline, reconnect, see the match and the teacher alert.

How it makes money

Core learning and matching stay free for students forever. Revenue comes from those who need to reach and measure them.

  • B2G licences: per-student yearly fee to state education and skill departments for dashboards and outcome reports
  • CSR sponsorship: companies adopt a district and get audited impact reports
  • Employers: pay per verified apprentice or hire, or per listing
  • Module marketplace: each teacher chooses free, pay per module, or monthly subscription; Raahi keeps about 20% of paid sales
  • Mentor and corporate onboarding: companies pay to list internships and run mentoring programmes
  • Premium: low-cost exam test series for students who can pay

Illustration of the licence model: one state licensing 5 lakh students at ₹60 per student per year = ₹3 crore a year. Price is an assumption to test in the pilot.

Trust, ethics and safety

  • No synthetic data anywhere: every dataset is publicly accessible and cited
  • AI answers only from verified data, always cites a source, and says “ask your teacher” when unsure
  • Career suggestions audited so girls see STEM and vocational paths equally
  • No ads to minors; only verified jobs and schemes, to block fraud
  • DPDP Act: recorded parental consent for under-18s, data minimisation, data stored in India, delete my data by voice
  • Safety filter on AI chat, with teacher alert for risky messages

Edge cases we design for

  • Five siblings on one phone: photo-avatar profiles, separate progress
  • Phone with under 1 GB free: optional packs, size shown before download
  • Power cut mid-quiz: every answer autosaved
  • Scheme deadline passes while offline: “may be outdated” tag on stale data
  • Speech not understood in a dialect: falls back to picture choices
  • Same profile used on two phones: progress events merged without loss
  • Student types or says something unsafe: filtered, teacher notified
  • Teacher leaves the school: class transfers to the next facilitator

Data rules we follow

  • Every record stores its source link and the date it was fetched; the app shows this to the user
  • Licences are respected, including the Government Open Data Licence – India and Creative Commons terms
  • If a dataset is missing a field, the app shows “not available” rather than filling it with generated values
  • Stale records are flagged and re-fetched on a schedule

No synthetic data in models or demo

  • Career matching starts as content-based matching over O*NET, NCO and PLFS data, not a model trained on fake students. It learns from real usage only after the pilot, with consent.
  • Speech accuracy is measured on public test sets such as Common Voice and Kathbath.
  • Demo dashboard uses real public district statistics from UDISE+ and ASER, plus real test sessions recorded by the team. No invented student records.

How a spoken question travels

  1. Student speaks in Hindi; the phone turns it into text offline.
  2. The intent model decides: app action, quiz answer, or knowledge question.
  3. Knowledge question: check the local answer cache first.
  4. No network: save it to the outbox and tell the student it's queued.
  5. Online: the AI service retrieves matching schemes or lessons, the LLM answers with sources, the safety filter checks it.
  6. Answer comes back as text and audio, and is stored on the phone for replay.

Sync, security and scale

  • Sync: progress stored as append-only events, so two phones merge without conflicts; profile fields use last-write-wins; content packs are versioned and only changes download
  • Bandwidth: daily sync aimed at a few hundred KB; large packs move over Wi-Fi or the village mesh
  • Security: OAuth2 tokens tied to the device, encrypted local database, TLS in transit, role-based access for student, teacher and organisation, audit logs
  • Scale: stateless services autoscale on Kubernetes, CDN serves packs, the shared answer cache cuts LLM cost as users grow
  • Extensible: new languages, careers and schemes are added as data and packs, not code; open-source repo with contributor guide

How it works without internet

Low-bandwidth engine and AI models

Rural internet is usually there but slow and patchy, not missing. Raahi is built for that. On slow 2G, Didi still uses the full cloud AI, because the phone turns speech into text itself and sends only a few KB. When the signal drops completely, the app keeps working on its own and makes no API calls.

Data budget on slow 2G (50 kbps)

ActionDataTime at 50 kbps
Ask Didi (text up, text down)~2 KB~0.3 s + AI time
Daily sync< 300 KB~48 s, in background
8-min audio lesson (16 kbps)~960 KB~2.6 min, prefetched
Scheme or deadline updatea few KBinstant

Voice bots that upload audio send 5–80 times more data per question. Raahi never uploads voice.

How Raahi stays fast on slow networks

  • Speech-to-text on the phone; only text travels
  • Answer text streams back, so Didi starts speaking within seconds
  • Compressed JSON, no images in API replies, short timeouts with retry
  • Lessons prefetched at night on charge, on school Wi-Fi, or from a classmate's phone
  • Resumable downloads that continue after the signal drops
  • Everything a student needs today is already on the phone, so slow internet never blocks a screen

When there is no signal at all, the app makes no API calls at all, and it doesn't need any. The phone is its own backend: every screen reads from the database on the phone, and small engines inside the app do the work a server would do. The cloud server is used only to sync, fetch fresh data and answer new questions.

The backend inside the phone

  1. Screens (Jetpack Compose) never call the internet directly.
  2. Repository layer answers every request from the local database. This is Google's recommended offline-first pattern: the phone's data is the single source of truth.
  3. Local engines written in Kotlin: eligibility rules engine, career scorer, search over the answer bank, quiz scoring, progress and streaks.
  4. Encrypted database (Room on SQLite with SQLCipher) holds content packs, schemes, careers, profiles and progress.
  5. Sync engine (WorkManager) quietly sends saved changes and pulls updates when any network appears.

Why no LLM on low-end phones

  • Google says its on-device LLM API is optimised for high-end phones like the Pixel 8 and Galaxy S23, and the model is too large to bundle in an app.
  • A 1B model takes hundreds of MB and would crowd a 2–3 GB phone.
  • Very small models make mistakes in Hindi and can invent schemes that don't exist.
  • So offline correctness comes from verified data, not generated text. The phone gets an answer bank: answers to common questions, written by the cloud LLM from public data, checked by a person, each citing its source. They're answers shipped in packs, never used as training data.

Honest limits

  • Offline speech exists today for Hindi, Gujarati and Telugu, and Telugu's small model is weak. In other languages the app works offline by tapping and pictures, with voice when online.
  • New questions that aren't in the answer bank wait for network.
  • Applications must be submitted on official portals; Raahi prepares everything and links straight to the form.

Problem
1) Learning does not lead to earning: only 5.6% of rural youth aged 14–18 take any vocational course (ASER 2023) and youth unemployment is 10.2% (PLFS 2023-24). Students don't know what they are good at or which career fits them. 2) Scholarships, schemes, apprenticeships, mentors and jobs sit on separate, English-first portals, so eligible students never apply. 3) Existing tools don't fit village reality: 25% of rural youth can't read a Class 2 text fluently, only 19.8% of rural girls own a smartphone (ASER 2023), there are 48 rural internet subscriptions per 100 people (TRAI 2026), and 1,04,125 schools have a single teacher (UDISE+ 2024-25).
Existing Alternatives
DIKSHA and SWAYAM for learning content; National Scholarship Portal, myScheme, NAPS and NCS portals for scholarships, schemes, apprenticeships and jobs; YouTube; paid coaching in towns; cyber-café agents who charge to fill forms; advice from relatives. Each solves one slice, mostly in English, needs internet, and nothing connects what a student learns to what they can earn.
Solution
Raahi: a voice-first, offline-first Android app with Didi, an AI helper character who talks in the student's language. MVP: (1) Discover: voice or text chat builds a profile of interests, skills and goals and suggests careers across STEM, vocational and non-STEM from the full NCO-2015 occupation list. (2) Learn: personal pathway, offline micro-lessons, relevant competitive exams, AI doubt help. (3) Opportunity: AI grounded in public data matches students to scholarships, schemes, apprenticeships, mentors and employers, and Didi helps fill the application. (4) Facilitator mode: one teacher runs a class on shared phones, spots talent, recommends students, and builds modules offered free, per module or by subscription.
Key Metrics
Pilot targets: 60% weekly active learners; 40% 30-day retention; 50%+ of sessions done offline; 3+ lessons per learner per week; 30% of eligible students start a scholarship or apprenticeship application and 60% of those submit. Also tracked: placements and mentor matches, active facilitators and their module earnings, cost per learner per year.
Unique Value Proposition
Speak in your own language, learn even on slow 2G or when the signal drops, and find the scholarships, apprenticeships and jobs you qualify for. One app, one mic button, and Didi to help you every step.
High Level Concept
Duolingo + myScheme + Naukri, voice-first, for a village student on a ₹6,000 phone.
Unfair Advantage
Village mesh sharing: one phone downloads a content pack and the whole class gets it over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct at zero data cost. A growing library of teacher-recorded lessons in local dialects. A verified scheme and scholarship knowledge base with eligibility rules and sources. Outcome data from learning to placement that governments and CSR funders need.
Channels
Outbound: government schools and ITIs through teachers, state education and skill departments, NGO and CSR partners, Common Service Centres and Panchayats. Inbound: WhatsApp referral links, phone-to-phone sharing between students, Play Store, preload deals with budget phone brands.
Customer Segments
Users: rural students from Class 9 to graduation, including ITI students, graduates and dropouts looking for work; village teachers and facilitators. Payers: state education and skill departments, CSR foundations, employers and apprenticeship providers, NGOs. Partners: mentors, corporates and NGOs onboarding to offer guidance and opportunities. Early adopters: government school teachers in one district who already run class WhatsApp groups.
